# 0. READ THIS FIRST — WHAT DALIA ACTUALLY WANTS

Improve the **existing** MPP Business Hub, not build a different product. Preserve existing project management, daily employee reviews, AI observations, approved files, notes, notifications, owner-only business intelligence, project pauses, and safe timekeeping integration boundaries.

The business owner wants a calm, trustworthy desktop experience with **strong attention hierarchy**, not a colorful or flashy dashboard. Users should know immediately where to look, what matters, and what to click next. Decorative project photos, unnecessary icons, repeated status pills, oversized colored circles, and many equally prominent cards undermine this goal.

Three essential changes:

1. **Print statistics should focus on only three primary performance measures:** (a) revenue for the selected day, (b) revenue per *productive* hour, and (c) number of orders printed **if actually verified**; otherwise explicitly label the figure **orders claimed**. **Prints per hour is no longer a priority.**
2. **The owner wants to see EVERY print-team member together for a selected day**, then move to yesterday, the day before, and earlier dates; selecting an employee opens that employee's detail without losing the team's context. Employees primarily see **their own** statistics on workplace PCs.
3. **Projects must use genuine drill-down navigation:** **All Projects → Project Steps → Selected Step Details**. Files, notes, discussion, evidence, actions, and updates belong to **individual steps**, **not** general project-wide Files or Notes pages. The project-level page is primarily a clean list of steps.

Additionally, add a **separate customer-service order-to-print-log investigation**. A CS rep searches an order number, finds the associated claim and personalization/file name, and inspects matching raw Windows print-log events to understand how many prints the computer submitted. Do not confuse recorded print events with physical delivered quantity.

**PC use:** Team members work on desktop PCs. The **owner alone** needs a mobile-oriented management view for reviews, decisions, messages, and status checks. Do not redesign the full employee desktop workspace around mobile restrictions.

## Priority and non-negotiable instructions

- **Do not rebuild from scratch, remove existing features, or perform broad visual redesigns unrelated to this brief.** Refactor the established screens/components and retain historical data and access paths.
- **Do not change the existing live Firebase clock-in/out system or production Firestore, Google Sheets, local MT Log collection, customer records, or order systems** during this design/implementation phase. Inspect read-only if authorized; provide separate integration and deployment plans.
- Follow an **existing, previously agreed revenue-per-hour calculation** supplied by Dalia to Claude. Retrieve it from the existing project/spec/code and quote its rule back in the implementation notes. **This document does not invent or replace that formula.**

- AI/MT-Log observations are evidence, not verified completion. Keep separate **observed**, **employee reported**, **verified**, and **approved** states.

# 5. REVENUE & PRODUCTIVE-HOUR DATA CONTRACT (DO NOT GUESS)

This is one of the highest-risk correctness areas. Dalia has **already** explained the calculation to Claude. Find the current approved requirement in the code/spec; **do not silently define a new rule**.

## Required computation and provenance

For each employee and selected business date, the service should expose:

- `eligible_revenue_amount` and `revenue_source`, eligible order IDs/claim IDs, timestamps, attribution basis and currency.
- `eligible_productive_minutes` and `time_source`, with underlying clock-in/out segments and deducted eligible project-work segments (subject to role authorization).
- `hourly_revenue = eligible_revenue_amount / (eligible_productive_minutes / 60)` only when the denominator is valid and greater than zero.
- `unique_orders_claimed`, plus `verified_orders_printed` only when verified source coverage permits that metric.
- `source_freshness`, `sync_status`, `completeness`, and `confidence/verification` metadata.

Use the **existing** owner-approved rules to determine which project time is deducted, how multiple shifts are combined, how breaks are handled, and where revenue is attributed. Review and preserve them, and document the exact sources.

### Important edge cases

- Overlapping project-time intervals must **not** be double-subtracted.
- Never subtract hours already excluded as off-clock/unpaid breaks.
- Work spanning midnight must be allocated to the correct **America/Chicago** business dates by the approved rule.
- Corrections to clock records and reassigned/returned/canceled orders must be reconciled according to approved source semantics. Do not count the same order's revenue twice.
- If hours are 0/missing/unverified, **display `—`, not `$0/hr` or infinity**. If revenue data is missing, display `Unavailable`, not `$0`.
- For current-day partially synced data, show `As of [timestamp]` and a provisional indicator.
- If some employees have valid hours and others don't, **do not silently divide full-team revenue by only the subset of hours**. Either (a) compute the rate from the same eligible cohort of employees with complete matched revenue/time or (b) suppress the team rate and state coverage, as the approved policy requires. Provide the cohort calculation and warning.
- When a user selects an earlier day, do not mix historical revenue with the current shift's hours.
- `Total Subtotal` in the Claims/Totals tables is not necessarily net marketplace payout, profit, or cash received. Use the exact approved revenue definition and label it faithfully.

### Calculation acceptance checks

1. One employee: 8 clocked hours, 2 approved project-work hours (if defined as deductible), $600 eligible revenue → 6 productive hours → `$100/hr` (illustrative fixture only).
2. Overlapping project segments: ensure the union, not raw sum, is subtracted.
3. Zero productive hours → no numeric hourly rate.
4. Team: Employee A `$300 / 3h = $100/h`, Employee B `$100 / 2h = $50/h`; team `$400 / 5h = $80/h`, **not** arithmetic mean `$75/h`.
5. Missing time for one employee → follow documented cohort/coverage rule and warn; never silently inflate the rate.
6. Duplicate claim rows or one order split across several lines → count and attribute as per unique-order business logic, without duplicate subtotal credits.

# 6. CUSTOMER SERVICE — ORDER → PERSONALIZATION/FILE → RAW PRINT LOG

This is a **new complementary capability**, separate from Print Team daily performance. Owner authorizes **specific customer-service permissions**. The purpose is investigating shortage complaints: e.g., customer purchased 40 invitations but says 30 arrived.

## Correct workflow

1. CS rep opens `Customer Service → Print Verification` (or an equally obvious entry point, authorized roles only).
2. Enters **exact order number** (e.g., an Amazon order ID); search must support marketplace/source context if identifiers can collide.
3. App searches the authorized order-claim data (`Claims` and/or verified native claim database), returning **order ID, claimant, SKU, purchased/claimed time, quantity, and claim subtotal where permitted**.
4. App retrieves the order's **actual personalization/name and production file reference** from an authorized linked order/production system. **The current workbook does not demonstrate that source exists.** Do not infer filenames from buyer names.
5. App identifies one or more candidate file names/path variants and matches against the relevant **raw print log** records (time window, employee/computer/printer, normalized filename, order ID if embedded, job metadata, and applicable workflow evidence).
6. App displays **print-job evidence**: observed file name, print submit time, workstation, printer if logged, event IDs, raw copies/pages/sheets, interpretation method, and any warnings.
7. CS rep reviews the evidence, optionally records a factual investigation note, and can copy a succinct evidence-based finding into an existing case workflow; this is **not** automatically a refund decision.

## UI organization — separate page; not an owner print-stats panel

 ...             ...           ...          ...                ...                 ...

### Raw-log safety / matching rules

**Do NOT use a naive filename substring match as final proof.** The same personal name may appear in multiple customer orders or repeats, filenames may change during proofing, different printers may log page/copy units differently, and the same job might be resent.

- Establish a typed ingestion/parser contract for **each real text log format** before implementation; retain immutable original log content, timestamps, machine, paths and parsed fields.
- Match exact normalized full filename/path where possible, plus time/source/customer context. Generate ranked candidates with rationale; never merge ambiguous candidates silently.
- Multiple print jobs can belong to one order and a print job may print several different items (cards, envelopes, certificates). Separate each product/component and **units**. A log value `40 pages`, `40 copies`, `40 sides`, or `40 sheets` is not necessarily `40 invitations`.
- The same order can be produced on multiple PCs; aggregate only distinct jobs with correct units, dedupe ingestion retries and resubmission events.
- Display `Exact verified link`, `Probable match—needs confirmation`, `Several possible matches`, or `No reliable match` based on evidence, not invented confidence percentages.
- If log volume shows 40 submitted, **say `40 submitted to printer`**, not `40 delivered`. Failures, jams, reprints, spoilage, manual trimming, packing shortages and shipping loss can still occur.
- If a customer says 30 received, display customer claim as **reported**, not authoritative physical count.
- Unmatched/missing logs ≠ no printing; show coverage/freshness warning.
- Provide audit trail of searches, human file match selections, corrections and notes. CS personnel must not edit raw logs, clock time, revenue totals, or order-claim attribution.
- Minimize buyer-identifying details and make case information available only to authorized CS/owner users. Do not expose all employees' revenue per hour to CS.

### Example evidence logic

Customer ordered `40 cards`. A raw print log may show `40` copies submitted for the card file, while a separate envelope job may show `40` envelopes. These are separate print operations; they do not establish 40 finished/packed cards. If the event's unit cannot be reliably interpreted, show **`Print event found — quantity interpretation unavailable`**.

### CS acceptance scenarios

- Exact order claim and unique verified file mapping → show matched source records and raw job units.
- Filename shared by two orders → require additional distinguishing evidence or manual choice; never auto-confirm.
- Same file submitted three times → show each distinct job; avoid double-counting duplicate log entries.
- A card and envelope file exist → list separately.
- No order-to-file mapping → show claimant/order details but a clear `File name not linked yet` blocker, not a fake match.
- Missing print logs → `Insufficient log coverage`; never `0 printed`.
- CS user attempts owner financial or timekeeping data access → denied by backend.

# 7. PROJECTS — THREE-LEVEL HIERARCHICAL NAVIGATION

**Exact information architecture (IA):**

**LEVEL 1: ALL PROJECTS** → select a project →  
**LEVEL 2: THAT PROJECT'S STEPS** → select a step →  
**LEVEL 3: THAT STEP'S DETAIL WORKSPACE**.

Do not show all projects, all steps and a full detail inspector at once in three crowded panes. Each level gets meaningful room. On a wide PC a **step-detail screen may show a compact contextual step list on the left** for switching steps, with selected-step details taking most width; do not show the full All Projects list alongside it.

## LEVEL 1 — ALL PROJECTS (owner)

**Primary purpose:** Scan every authorized project at a glance, see progress and owner/assignee, and open the right one.

Layout:
- Page title `Projects` and a short description only if useful.
- Search by project name/ID/assigned person.
- Status filters `All | Needs attention | In progress | Paused | Completed`, with consistent actual counts. Support private/owner-only grouping without leaking it to employees.

## Role-specific step actions

- **Employee:** Read assigned step, open approved resources, update progress, add step-specific notes, report completion; cannot approve official versions or bypass owner checks.
- **Owner/authorized manager:** All of the above plus assign, reorder/edit steps, approve files/verification, request update, pause/resume project, reject/inquire about AI evidence; all actions journaled.
- **Files:** `Choose/use file` does not mean `Approve official version`. Explicit approval rights required.
- **Completion:** `Employee reported complete` is not `externally verified complete`. The project progress display must distinguish the source; otherwise the team sees misleading 3-of-4 completion counts.
- **Activity:** MT Log events may be linked to a step but missing events cannot mean no work. Ambiguous activity remains unconfirmed.

# 9. VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM — SPECIFIC IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

This section is the antidote to the "high-school project" feeling: **intentional hierarchy, consistent rhythm, fewer competing elements**, and quiet professional polish.

## Layout & attention

- On an employee PC (~1280–1920 px): fixed or collapsible **primary nav 190–235 px**; content fills remaining usable width. One consistent page heading and one meaningful primary CTA.
- At project-step level: optional context step list **260–320 px**; most width reserved for actual step work. Do not cram All Projects + Project Steps + Every Detail onto one screen.
- Use 8-px base spacing rhythm with consistent increments: **8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 px**; larger 40–48 px separation only between truly separate page sections.
- Control line length for long instructions and notes (roughly 60–80 characters per line where practical).
- Use consistent left-alignment, strong gutters, deliberate indentation to show nesting/relationship, generous row height, and clear content grouping.
- Prefer **simple rows with quiet dividers** over a new colored card for every item. Cards should separate meaningful regions, not decorate every value.
- Make one main focal point (selected step, today's performance summary, or needs-attention decision) obvious in **less than five seconds**.

## Typography

Recommended visual scale (responsive to browser zoom):
- Main page title: **26–30 px**, semibold, near-navy/charcoal.
- Selected task/step heading: **22–26 px**, semibold.
- Section labels: **16–18 px**, semibold.
- Body / table main data: **14–16 px**.
- Supporting metadata: **12–14 px**, lower contrast but still accessible.
- Numeric KPIs: **26–34 px** depending on emphasis; use tabular numerals for aligned money and counts.

Do not use five different typefaces/weights. Serif editorial display typography is optional only if it improves readability; practical desktop UI should favor a single highly legible sans-serif.

## Color rules

- Background: near-white, neutral warm tint; main content surfaces mostly white.
- Text: dark neutral; secondary text medium gray with accessible contrast.
- **MPP pink** = selected navigation, primary button, focus accent **sparingly**. Avoid coloring all KPI backgrounds and every row pink.
- Green = verified/positive; amber = needs attention/pending; red = error/blocker; gray = neutral; never use color alone to communicate state.
- Exactly **one prominent primary button per context** (e.g., `Report progress`, `New project`, `Search`), with quiet secondary links.
- Badges are small and consistent; omit repetitive pills where plain supporting text would suffice.

## Imagery & icons

- **Real employee avatars** are valuable: they let users identify people quickly. Show at a consistent small size (~28–36 px) and allow initials fallback.
- Do not add pretend product photos, stationery pictures, generic printers or decorative asset thumbnails on operational project rows.
- Show **real attachments' previews** only when materially useful to completing the step or investigating an order.
- Keep a consistent icon family and size; do not use circles/squares/illustrations around every label.

## Interaction details

- Clearly clickable rows (`hover`, keyboard focus, pointer, chevron where helpful) and explicit **Back** routes.
- One-click access to verified step resources; no "go to All Files" detours.
- Action confirmation only for consequential approval/deletion/status changes; undo where safe.
- Human-friendly error/empty states, not internal filenames/sequence numbers as headlines.
- Sorting/filtering preserves the user's selected date/employee/project and avoids sudden jumps.
- No unnecessary motion, auto-refresh jumps, toast overload or modal chains.
- Accessibility: keyboard navigation, visible focus, label controls, color contrast per WCAG, readable density at 125%/150% browser zoom.
